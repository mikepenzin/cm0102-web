Privacy

CM 01/02 in the Browser is an unofficial fan project. The game runs in your browser, on your device.

What this site keeps in your browser

To play, the page keeps in your browser's storage on your device: the game files (so they download once), your saved games, your data sets and the game's settings, and a few choices of the page (for example your answer about the statistics). They are needed for the game to work, stay on your device and are not sent anywhere; with Cloud Saves, your saves, data sets and tactics also go to your account (below). Without an account the site sets no cookies. Only if you allow the statistics does the page also keep a random id of your browser for them (next section).

Usage statistics (only if you allow them)

The page asks you once whether it may send them, with Allow and No thanks. Until you allow them it sends nothing for them and keeps no id for them. If you say no, only your answer goes to the server, with no id and nothing else, so that we can count how many people say yes and no. If you allow them: while the game runs, the page tells the server once a minute how long it was played (while the tab is visible and you use it) and how many saved games, older copies and data sets your browser keeps, with their total size. With it go a random id that your browser made for this site (not your name or email), the browser's name and system, for example "Chrome 142 · Mac", and the browser's language, for example "de-DE" (to learn which languages the page should speak). If you are signed in, the statistics count for your account, and the random id of the browser is linked to it. If the game stops on an error, its message and the last lines of the game's log go to the server too. No IP address is kept, and nothing of your saved games is sent.

We use the statistics to see how many people play and how much, and to find errors. They are not sold or given to anyone. A browser's statistics are deleted after 13 months without a visit; error reports after 90 days. You can change your answer any time in Help, Statistics; turning them off stops them at once and deletes the browser's random id from your browser.

Send to the developer (only when you press it)

After an error, and in Help, Send to the developer sends a report that you start yourself: the engine log of this visit (what the game wrote while it ran), the error's message, what you write in the box, the size of the page and the device's memory as the browser tells them, your browser's name and system, and the engine's version. If you choose a saved game in the dialog, that file goes too: it holds the game world and your manager's name as you typed it. With the statistics allowed, the browser's random id goes with the report, and when you are signed in, your account. No IP address is kept. We use reports only to find and fix errors; they are not sold or given to anyone, and the report and its saved game are deleted after 90 days. When a report comes, the developer gets a short notice through a messaging service: the error's message, your browser's name and system, the game's engine version, whether you wrote a note, and the name and size of the saved game you sent. What you wrote, the log and the saved game itself stay on this site.

With an account

You sign in with your Google account. Google tells this site:

Your Google account idA number that names your account; your saves are kept under it.
Your email addressTo show which account is signed in, and to give cloud saves to the accounts that have them.
Your name and pictureYour name is shown on the Account button. The picture is not used or kept.

The site asks Google for nothing else: not your contacts, your files or your mail. It keeps your account id, email address, name, the date you first and last signed in, and your plan.

Cloud saves

If your account has cloud saves, each saved game you make goes to the site's storage, with its file name, its size and the time it was saved; so do the data sets (your own copies of the game's database) that you make or upload, and the tactics you save on the tactics screen or upload (each file with its name and time, and the names of the tactics you deleted in Saved games with the time, so they go from your other devices too). The storage is Tigris, an object store of Fly.io, where this site runs. Nothing else of the game goes there. Your browser sends and gets these files from Tigris itself, with a link that this site makes for each file and that works for 15 minutes, so Tigris sees your browser's IP address as any web server does.

Payments

Membership is a membership on Buy Me a Coffee. You give your card or other payment details to Buy Me a Coffee and its payment processors, not to this site; Buy Me a Coffee's privacy policy covers them. Buy Me a Coffee tells this site about your membership: the email and name you joined with, the membership's id, level, amount and state (active, canceled, paused) and the end of the period you paid for. The site keeps the email, id, level, state and the end of the period, under a key made from the email, to know whether Membership is on; it keeps no name and no amount.

Cookies

A signed-in browser holds one cookie, cm_session, for 30 days: it says which account is signed in. During the sign-in a second cookie, cm_oauth, holds the state of the sign-in for at most 10 minutes. This site sets no other cookies, and has no ads and no tracking. Joining a membership happens on Buy Me a Coffee's own site, in another tab; its privacy policy covers it.

Deleting your data

Account, then Delete account, removes your account, all of your cloud saves and your account's usage statistics from the storage at once. The saves in your browser stay until you clear the site's data in the browser. Signing out removes the cookie from the browser. A running membership is canceled first (on Buy Me a Coffee); the deletion then removes the membership record too. Buy Me a Coffee keeps the records of your payments as the law asks of it.
